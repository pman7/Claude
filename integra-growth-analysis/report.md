Prepared for Phillip Manchik, Nova Dental LLC dba Integra Dental Design, Bellevue, WA

## 1. The direct recommendation

**Grow from the building you already pay for, and do not sign a lease anywhere. Run two things for the next 6 to 12 months: Eastside dentist accounts for implant and full-arch work (the primary engine), and lab-to-lab milling for Pacific Northwest labs under the Bear Milling name (the one complementary initiative). Treat equipment sales as business as usual: answer inbound inquiries the same day and close or kill the two open quotes, but stop prospecting. Finish the TRI matrix certification training, which is free, and do not pay the $13,785 FDA establishment registration TRI requires of certified labs until TRI answers four questions in writing (Section 12.1). Do not open an office in Orange County or Maui.**

Why this and not geographic expansion:

1. **The constraint is not capacity or geography. It is founder attention, cash and delivery reliability.** Your own August 2026 reconstruction shows 519 billed units in twelve months, roughly a day and a half of milling per month, two clients at 91 percent of lab revenue, cash that touched $4.73 on March 31 and $2,421 on August 27, and 6 of 13 active cases late or past their ship date. Every expansion model that adds a lease, a hire or a travel schedule makes all three constraints worse before it makes anything better.
2. **No lease-based option is positive after owner time in any scenario, and most do not fit the cash envelopes.** At $5,000, no office, satellite, showroom or production option fits; at $15,000 and $30,000 only the South Orange County consultation office fits on cash, and it still loses about $39,500 in its first year at base (Sections 7.4, 8.3). The honest caveat is that the remote options are also negative after owner time at base, and growing in place is itself at roughly breakeven in year one; the difference is that the remote and in-place options cost $1,000 to $8,000 to try and can be stopped in a month, while a lease commits $16,000 to $277,000 before the first case.
3. **Puget Sound is not an underserved market, and this report does not claim it is.** The research agent for this market concluded that the Eastside is "already served by roughly 30 named independent labs in King County alone plus national mail-order labs" and that "there is no evidence it is underserved"; no agent in any market found evidence of dentist dissatisfaction with incumbent labs. Washington and California have about the same number of lab establishments per dentist (175 labs for 7,071 licenses, one per 40; 734 for 30,280, one per 41). What Puget Sound has that no other market on the list has is your relationships (Dr. Jung, Dr. Keller, one trial account, Seattle Study Club's headquarters in Kirkland), zero shipping cost, zero new tax nexus, a chairside visit within 25 minutes, and the only path that reduces the two-client concentration. That is the case for growing in place; it is a case about your position, not about the market.
4. **Every distant market has a structural problem you cannot fix with an office.** South Orange County contains the world's largest dental lab (Glidewell, about a million square feet in Irvine and Newport Beach), a Laguna Hills lab offering four-hour same-day zirconia within ten miles, and in-house labs at the highest-value full-arch practices. Maui has about 116 licensed dentists, no independent crown-and-bridge lab, but four practices with captive digital labs, Oahu labs one flight away and mainland labs shipping in free by USPS at 2 to 4 days. Anchorage is reachable in two days by flat-rate FedEx and same day by air, but has four credible labs and in-house production at its two most aggressive implant practices. Dallas and Miami have no reason to prefer a Bellevue lab at all, and Texas would require a certified technician on premises in Texas.
5. **The two highest-value things you can sell are already in the building:** a full-arch zirconia protocol with a documented slow-cooldown fix, and an implant crown on a stock or OEM abutment at $269 to $299, about 16 percent under Glidewell's $339 screw-retained crown and 40 percent under its $475 to $486 crown on a custom abutment, which is a different product. Neither has been sold systematically. The All-on-X lines on your fee schedule have never been billed. And the mills are 90 percent idle, which is what makes lab-to-lab milling a near-zero-cost complement.

**What the ranking actually says, stated plainly.** After an adversarial review corrected the model (delivery was double-counted, founder hours were understated, commission was a flat line rather than a percentage, the evidence score for the Eastside was too generous), the weighted ranking in Section 11 puts Bear Milling (B) first at 3.30, Alaska remote (J) second at 3.20 and growing in place (A) third at 3.10, with every lease option in the bottom five. On the four quantitative criteria alone A ranks eighth, because it is at breakeven in year one after charging your time and needs more hours than the cheap remote options. A is first in the calendar for two reasons the scoring captures only partly: it is the only option that reduces the Jung concentration, and both A and B depend on the same first step, shipping on time. Alaska's second place is an artifact of costing almost nothing; it has no relationships and the agents found four credible Anchorage labs. If you weigh the numbers only, run B first. The no-lease conclusion is the same either way.

**The strongest argument against this recommendation.** The lab's unit economics are small. At $133 average per unit and about $11,700 a month collected, the base case for the two engines together adds about $19,600 a month of revenue by month 12 (six dentist accounts at $1,495, eight labs at $1,328) and about $3,600 a month of cash after paying you $60 an hour, and the first year as a whole is roughly breakeven after your time. That covers the business and starts to pay you; it does not change your life. Equipment does: one ARUM sale carries roughly $17,000 of gross margin, and your dealer rights have no territory restriction. If you believe you can close three to five machines in the next year, the equipment business is worth more than every lab option combined, and "grow in place" looks like a decision to stay small. The honest counter is that seven months of dealership produced zero closes and two stalled quotes, the Q4 plan's own base case was three to five machines with maximal effort, and equipment sales consume the same founder hours that the lab needs. So the recommendation is not "abandon equipment." It is: stop letting equipment prospecting set your calendar, answer inbound equipment heat the same day, close the two quotes already open, and let the lab fund the business while machines remain upside.

A second real objection: "grow in place" has been the plan since August and the pipeline shows a Kirkland list, a Bellevue list, one trial account (Dr. Joe Kim) and one new remote account (Harmony Dental, Eugene). If it has not worked in two months, why would it work now? Because it has not actually been run. The Eight Dentists plan called for eleven calls in one morning, sample arches milled that week and four drop-offs a day. The Maui trip, the Buffalo trip, the Q4 equipment plan and the TRI onboarding happened instead. Section 14 turns the plan into a 90-day schedule with weekly numbers and gates so that you can tell by week 4 whether it is working.

**Three compliance facts surfaced by this research that change the plan more than any market does.** (1) Your own October 7 Notion task records that TRI requires certified manufacturing centers to hold their own FDA establishment registration as a contract manufacturer (historical, from your record; not yet confirmed in writing by TRI). The FY2027 annual fee is $13,785 and the initial registration cannot be waived ([FDA FY2027 fee notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/30/2026-15335/medical-device-user-fee-rates-for-fiscal-year-2027)). That turns the TRI option from a $2,400 wedge into a $19,000 first-year commitment and moves it from second to ninth in the ranking; FDA practice on patient-specific implant components makes a waiver unlikely (Section 10). It is still worth doing once TRI's referral volume is known, and not before. (2) Washington requires every dental laboratory to register with the Department of Health and, for registrations issued after January 31, 2025, to employ a Certified Dental Technician working at least 30 hours a week or operate under a licensed dentist's supervision ([RCW 70.352.050](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=70.352.050)). You are registered (DLAB.FS.70096007, effective September 8, 2025); your CDT, Young Kim, is scheduled for 20 hours a week. Either the registration rests on dentist supervision, which should be documented, or Young's hours need to reach 30, which also happens to be the design capacity the growth plan needs. (3) Oregon now requires any lab preparing work from an Oregon dentist's work order to hold a Health Licensing Office authorization ($250 application plus $500 authorization, renewing at $750 a year, with material and point-of-origin disclosures); until 2030 the qualified-person test is met by any employee working 30 hours a week who completed 12 approved continuing-education hours in the prior year, so Edward Petruk (full time) plus 12 CE hours satisfies it without touching Young's schedule; enforcement discretion ends October 31, 2026 ([Oregon HLO permanent rule HLO 4-2026](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/PH/HLO/Temporary%20Blog%20Documents/DLP-Permanent_Rule_Filing-10-01-2026.pdf), [HLO program page](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/ph/hlo/pages/board-dental-laboratory-program.aspx)). Harmony Dental in Eugene is an Oregon account.

## 3. How the options were evaluated

Fourteen geography / service / customer combinations were modeled the same way:

- **Operating models compared separately** for each geography where they apply: remote service from Bellevue; periodic sales visits and partnerships; a client consultation / pickup / case-coordination office; a staffed finishing and QC satellite; a production lab; an equipment demonstration and training location. A mailing address or coworking desk is not treated as a production lab anywhere in this document, and the marketing implications of each footprint are noted.
- **Costs** are itemized low / base / high in `model/inputs.json`: rent, NNN or operating pass-throughs, deposits (shown separately and included in cash required), build-out, utilities, internet, insurance, permits, entity registration and franchise taxes, staffing with payroll load, owner time at an explicit opportunity cost of $40 / $60 / $100 an hour, travel (flights, cars, lodging, meals), shipping both ways by lane, remakes, payment fees and marketing. Bellevue's existing overhead is excluded to avoid double counting. Equipment purchase and financing are shown as alternatives, not added together, and no financing approval is assumed.
- **Demand** is modeled as accounts ramping linearly to a 12-month count, times units per account per month by product, times contribution per unit on that market's shipping lane. Formulas are in Section 8.
- **Ranking** weights year-1 profit after owner time (25), cash to open plus 90 days (20), month-12 cash flow per owner hour (15), exit exposure (10), capability fit (15), evidence quality (10) and speed to first revenue (5). Three alternative weightings and a quantitative-only score test sensitivity (Section 11).
- **Which model-geography pairs were modeled and which were not.** South Orange County gets all six operating models because it is the market you asked about most specifically and the one where a lease is a live idea. Maui gets remote and a coordination office; a Maui finishing satellite or production lab was not modeled because the island market (64 payroll dental offices, four captive labs) cannot support the staffed footprints that already fail in Orange County on larger numbers. Alaska, DFW and Miami get remote only, for the same reason. Puget Sound gets grow-in-place only: a second Seattle or Tacoma pickup office would add rent to a market you can already drive across, and a Bellevue equipment demo room already exists in the sense that matters (the A3 demo unit is in the building and can be shown to any prospect). Those exclusions are judgments, stated here so they can be challenged.

## 4. Market-by-market findings

### 4.1 Puget Sound baseline (King, Snohomish, Pierce)

**Demand.** Washington had 6,886 dentist licenses in 2022 and 7,071 in 2024, about 75 per 100,000 residents statewide in 2023. The King County region ("Healthier Here" ACH) had 112 licensed dentists per 100,000 in 2023, up from 109 in 2016; every other region in the state was under 70 ([UW CHWS, current supply](https://familymedicine.uw.edu/chws/resources/wohw/workforce-data/current-supply/), fact; license mailing address, not practice location). Applied to King County's 2,411,700 residents ([OFM April 1, 2025](https://ofm.wa.gov/sites/default/files/public/dataresearch/pop/april1/ofm_april1_press_release.pdf)) that is about 2,700 licensed dentists, roughly 2,100 of them general practitioners (78.8 percent GP share, [ADA HPI 2025](https://www.ada.org/-/media/project/ada-organization/ada/ada-org/files/resources/research/hpi/US_dentist_workforce_2025.pdf); estimate). The Seattle-King County Dental Society reports more than 1,600 members; WSDA took over its management on September 24, 2026. Within a 25-minute drive of the lab (Bellevue, Kirkland, Redmond, Issaquah, Sammamish, Mercer Island, Renton, Bothell) that is roughly 600 to 700 licensed dentists and perhaps 350 to 450 practice locations (estimate from the UW density figure applied to about 600,000 Eastside residents at 1.5 to 2 dentists per location; the Kirkland and Bellevue shortlists in your Notion already name 30 of them). The Bel-Red corridor within about a mile of your door holds a cluster worth naming: prosthodontists Dr. Siamak Najafi (14645 Bel-Red Rd) and Dr. Warren Libman (14595 Bel-Red Rd, in the same complex as Prime Dental Lab), Lifetime Smiles (Dr. Michael Beke, same-day All-on-X, 2800 156th Ave SE), Cascadia Dental Specialists (periodontists placing All-on-X, 14700 NE 8th St) and Bellevue Dental Arts (iTero Lumina, 13333 Bel-Red Rd); further out, Overlake Dental (Dr. Monika Yu, same-day All-on-4), Eastside Prosthodontics (Dr. Rosario Palacios), Mercer Island Dental (Dr. Goichi Shiotsu, past president of the Washington State Society of Prosthodontists) and Kirkland Premier Dentistry (five dentists including a prosthodontist and an oral surgeon, All-on-4 placed and restored in-house). Michael and Russell Johnson) appears affiliated with PNW Pros Dental Lab, so its lab work may be captive; qualify before investing a visit.

**Who buys outside labs.** The ADA Clinical Evaluators Panel found 53 percent of dentists using intraoral scanners and cost the main barrier for the rest ([cited by The Dental Signal, Mar 2026](https://www.thedentalsignal.com/cerec-in-office-milling-roi/), fact as cited). **Competition.** The research agent for this market put it bluntly: the three-county market is "already served by roughly 30 named independent labs in King County alone plus national mail-order labs, DSO in-house labs, and full-arch chains with on-site labs. There is no evidence it is underserved," and it found no evidence of dentist dissatisfaction with existing labs ("claims of unmet need would be speculation"). This report accepts that. Glidewell's Next-Day BruxZir service means "24-hour turnaround" is not by itself a differentiator for single scanned crowns; the chairside visit, the implant and arch work, and the local hand-off are. The honest obstacle is that this market has heard "local digital lab" before; the trial case and the arch in the doctor's hand are what move it.

**Costs to grow here.** No new premises. The model's base case is $5,350 of one-time spend (sample arches and case-study kit, scanner-directory listings, a CE evening, the Oregon lab authorization that keeps the Eugene account legal, and a deposit on a PNDC 2027 booth, April 1 to 3, 2027, Seattle Convention Center) and about $890 a month of cash cost (modest ads, prospecting mileage, society dues, and an allowance for the Medit loaner scanner you advertise) plus a commission of about 7 percent of new revenue to a rep or canvasser once accounts exist. Dental lab sales reps in current postings earn 6 to 8 percent of collected revenue, sometimes with a 3 percent residual in later years ([Ultra Core Dental Labs posting](https://connections.villanova.edu/jobs/ultra-core-labs-dental-sales-representative/); [DCS Dental Lab posting](https://www.ihire.com/jobs/view/500344993), read Oct 12, 2026). Seattle-metro dental laboratory technicians earn a median $27.70 an hour (BLS OEWS May 2025, via [WageDex](https://wagedex.com/jobs/dental-laboratory-technicians/metro/seattle-tacoma-bellevue-wa)), relevant when you add design hours. Washington classifies dental laboratories under L&I risk class 3602-05 ("Dental shops, laboratories, or kiosks", [WAC 296-17A-3602](https://app.leg.wa.gov/WAC/default.aspx?cite=296-17A-3602)); the 2026 composite base rate for class 3602 is $0.4056 per hour, of which $0.13195 is the employee's share, so the employer's net cost is about $0.27 per hour, roughly $570 a year per full-time technician ([L&I 2026 rates by class](https://www.lni.wa.gov/insurance/_docs/2026RatesByClassCodeFund.pdf)). Dental labs are manufacturers for B&O purposes and sales of dental prostheses to dentists are exempt from Washington sales tax under [RCW 82.08.0283](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=82.08.0283) (facts; your DOR B&O inquiry already resolved at effectively zero owed). Washington also requires every dental laboratory doing business in the state to register with the Department of Health; registrations issued after January 31, 2025 must document a Certified Dental Technician working at least 30 hours a week or operation under a licensed dentist's supervision, and the lab must maintain that qualified person; renewal is $250 a year ([DOH dental laboratories](https://doh.wa.gov/licenses-permits-and-certificates/facilities-z/dental-laboratories), [RCW 70.352.050](https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=70.352.050)). Integra is registered (DLAB.FS.70096007, effective September 8, 2025; your Notion lists the expiry as September 7, 2027 while DOH describes annual July 31 renewals, which should be reconciled). Young Kim is a CDT scheduled for 20 hours a week, so the 30-hour condition deserves a check (Section 10).

### 4.2 South Orange County (Aliso Viejo, Laguna Niguel, Laguna Beach, Laguna Hills, Mission Viejo, Dana Point, San Juan Capistrano)

**Demand.** The Orange County Dental Society has 2,530 member dentists (January 2025) and is the largest of California's 32 components ([OCDS profile](https://ce.edu.dental/orange-county-dental-society-ocds/)); total practicing dentists in the county are estimated at roughly 2,500 to 3,000 (OCDS membership plus an 8 percent population share of California's 30,280 active dentists, [Dentx, June 2026](https://dentx.ca/blog/orange-county-dental-market-report/), estimate). South County is affluent and implant-heavy; the practices that would value your protocol exist in numbers.

**Competition, and why this is the hardest market on the list.** California has 734 dental lab establishments and 9,086 lab employees, more than any other state (Census CBP 2023 via PlainBizBench, fact). Orange County is the center of that: Glidewell's headquarters and main production are in Irvine and Newport Beach (2201 Dupont Dr, Irvine), the Garden Grove / Westminster / Santa Ana corridor holds dozens of small Vietnamese-American and family labs with very low prices (directory evidence only; no county census count was obtainable), Alien Milling is in Glendora, and LMT LAB DAY West (131 exhibitors, 1,600 attendees, May 15 to 16, 2026; next edition May 14 to 15, 2027, Hyatt Regency Orange County, Garden Grove) is held there precisely because the lab industry is. ARUM Dentistry USA is at 17952 Sky Park Circle, Irvine, and TRI's US Digital Hub is at 2850 Red Hill Ave, Santa Ana. A dentist in Laguna Niguel can get a same-day pickup from a dozen labs and next-morning delivery from Glidewell.

**Taxes and entity rules that an office triggers.** California treats dental laboratories as retailers: "Tax applies to their entire charges for such products regardless of whether a separate charge or billing is made for materials and manufacturing services" ([CDTFA Regulation 1506(e)](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutr/1506.html), Business Taxes Law Guide revision 2025, fact). A physical office creates nexus, so every California sale would carry Orange County's 7.75 percent sales tax. California labs already charge it, so dentists will accept it on the invoice, but it is a compliance layer (seller's permit, quarterly returns) you do not have today. "Every LLC that is doing business or organized in California must pay an annual tax of $800"; the first-year exemption applied only to tax years 2021 through 2023 ([FTB](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/business/types/limited-liability-company/index.html), updated Mar 5, 2026, fact). Registering Nova Dental LLC as a foreign LLC (Form LLC-5) is $70 plus a $20 Statement of Information. Without an office, a Washington lab shipping into California stays below the $500,000 economic-nexus threshold and owes nothing in California. One nuance from CDTFA Regulation 1591: crowns, bridges, dentures and inlays are explicitly taxable, but dental implant systems including abutments sold to a dentist for treatment are exempt medicines, so a separately stated abutment line would be exempt ([Reg. 1591](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutr/1591.html)). California has no dental-lab registration statute; labs operate under the Dental Practice Act exemption in Business and Professions Code 1626(e) with a dentist's signed written authorization (agent finding, medium confidence).

## 6. Why a customer would switch to you, and what stops them

**Dentist-facing lab demand.** Dentists switch labs on fit and remake pain, turnaround misses, communication, and a specific clinical problem solved; price alone moves the bottom of the market (this is the industry pattern as reported in trade sources, not measured data for your market; none of the research agents found evidence of dentist dissatisfaction with incumbent labs in Puget Sound, Orange County or Maui, so the switching rates below are estimates). Your levers, in order of credibility: (1) a sample arch and a documented protocol in the doctor's hand; (2) an implant crown package about 16 percent under Glidewell's like-for-like screw-retained crown, with a fit guarantee; (3) a 10-minute drive and a technician who will come to the chair; (4) TRI certification for the TRI users. The barriers: a bad first case (your late-ship rate), the office manager who owns the lab relationship, existing lab programs bundled with implant companies, and the fact that most practices use two labs and will give you the overflow first. Expect a 15 to 25 percent trial-to-recurring conversion on warm introductions and well under 10 percent on cold outreach (estimate; your own Dr. Joe Kim and Harmony Dental experience is the local evidence).

**Lab-to-lab milling demand.** Labs switch milling partners on price, cutoff time and whether the milling center competes for their doctors. Your levers: a Pacific cutoff, "we never contact your doctors," no design or intake fee, and a sintering protocol for arches. Barriers: Haus and Alien are cheaper on crowns, every lab already has a milling partner, and your own ops record (late cases) is the opposite of what a milling customer buys. Conversion from a first case is high (labs are rational buyers) if the first case ships on time.

**Equipment and software demand.** Labs and practices buy mills on trust in the installer, financing, and a deadline. Your levers: operator credibility, dealer rights across brands (ARUM, MillBox, hyperDENT), and the Section 179 calendar (2026 limit $2,560,000 with a $4,090,000 phase-out and permanent 100 percent bonus depreciation for property acquired after January 19, 2025; the asset must be placed in service by December 31 for a 2026 deduction, [IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32, section 4.24](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf); bonus depreciation per P.L. 119-21 section 70301; confirm with the buyer's CPA). Barriers: Axsys's service depth, ARUM USA selling in its own backyard, your lack of a stocked spare-parts kit and a second installer, and the cash float on financed deals (Ascentium and Geneva fund at acceptance, after installation).

## 8. Profitability, capacity and downside

### 8.1 Formulas (implemented in `model/model.py`)

- Accounts in month t = A12 × min(1, t / ramp months), where A12 is the 12-month account count (low / base / high) and ramp is 6 to 12 months.
- Units in month t by product = accounts(t) × units per account per month for that product (the "case mix" in `inputs.json`; for option A the base mix per account is 4 zirconia crowns, 2 implant crowns, 0.2 full arches and 0.8 night guards a month, weighted toward the work the offer leads with).
- Revenue(t) = Σ units × price.
- Contribution per unit = price − materials − labor hours × loaded technician rate ($32 / $40 / $48) − round-trip shipping on that market's lane ÷ units per shipment − payment fee (0 / 3 / 3.5 percent) − remake allowance (3 / 5 / 8 percent of materials, labor and shipping, because a remake repeats them). Pessimistic contribution uses the low price with high costs; optimistic the reverse.
- Sales commission (option A only) = 5 / 7 / 8 percent of revenue, subtracted from contribution; break-even accounts use contribution net of commission.
- Option cash flow(t) = Σ units × contribution − monthly option cash cost. Fully loaded cash flow subtracts owner hours × $40 / $60 / $100.
- Break-even accounts = monthly option cash cost ÷ contribution per account (cash basis), or (monthly cash + owner time) ÷ contribution per account (fully loaded).
- Payback month = first month in which cumulative option cash flow covers cash to open. Maximum drawdown = the most negative cumulative cash position, including the pre-revenue outlay. A prepaid month of rent is in cash to open and is not charged again in month 1. "First positive month" is the first month the fully loaded monthly figure is positive.
- Capacity check: Σ units × labor hours ≤ 220 spare production hours a month (Young about 80 design hours, Edward about 160 CAM and mill hours, part-time finishing about 120 hours, less about 70 hours used by current work; estimate). Spindle time is not binding at roughly 90 percent idle. Design is the tighter sub-constraint: Young's 20 scheduled hours a week are about 87 a month, of which current work (about 43 units) uses roughly 40, leaving about 45 spare design hours; at roughly a third of labor hours being design, the high cases of A and B exhaust design time around month 6 to 8, before the 220-hour total does (Section 8.5).
- Stress tests (Section 8.4) rerun the model: half the accounts; the remake rate doubled through the unit economics; all monthly option costs plus 25 percent; the zirconia crown price matched to Glidewell's $109; one average account lost; and the ramp slowed by three months (reported as year-1 profit, since month-12 cash flow does not depend on timing).

Direct labor is charged per unit at the loaded technician rate; your own hours are charged separately at the opportunity-cost rate and never double counted with labor. Bellevue overhead is not allocated.

### 8.5 Capacity, bottlenecks and the cost of distraction

The binding constraints, in order: (1) design hours, one senior designer at about 20 hours a week; (2) finishing and QC labor, part-time; (3) your own hours, 45 a month for the dentist engine averaged over the year (59 in the launch quarter), 25 for Bear Milling and 10 for inbound equipment; (4) cash, which touched $4.73 and $2,421 this year. Spindle time is not a constraint and will not be until the lab triples. In the high case of A (about 200 labor hours a month at month 12) you are at the 220-hour ceiling and, on the design sub-constraint of about 45 spare hours, out of design time by month 6 to 8, so you should be contracting design by month 6; in the high case of B (795 units, 280 hours) you need a second CAM and finishing person. The practical implication: the first hire this business should make is a part-time designer or a commission salesperson, not anyone in another city.

The distraction cost is measurable. Thirteen active cases with six late and six past ship date (August 27) happened while the founder was on equipment calls and travel. The dentist engine, Bear Milling and inbound equipment together need about 80 founder hours a month (45 plus 25 plus 10) plus the operations hours that the late cases say are already missing. That is why Section 13 caps the equipment business at inbound and the two open quotes, and why the model charges 45 hours a month to A even though the plan's launch quarter runs at 59.

### 8.6 Do partnerships and remote service capture most of the benefit without a lease?

Yes, in every market where the question was asked. South OC remote (D) versus office (E): the office adds about $24,000 of first-year loss for two extra accounts at base. Maui remote (H) versus office (I): the office adds about $63,000 of loss for one extra account. Equipment national (M) versus a South OC showroom (N): the showroom adds about $41,000 of loss for a 0.05-per-month increase in close rate, in the OEM's own backyard. In each pair the lease version also carries 12 to 36 months of exit exposure that the remote version does not. The only thing a physical presence buys in these markets is a conversion lift, and nothing in the evidence supports a lift large enough to pay for it. Where presence genuinely matters (a shade visit, a fit check, a chairside rescue) you already have it, within 25 minutes of the lab.

Your October 7 Notion task records that TRI requires certified manufacturing centers to register as contract manufacturers (historical, unconfirmed by TRI in writing); TRI's own matrix clearance (K203660, cleared as an endosseous implant line under 21 CFR 872.3640) describes the matrix components as manufactured at TRI facilities from a design file, which is why a lab milling onto them is a contract-manufacturing question; the FY2027 annual establishment registration fee is $13,785 (October 1, 2026 to September 30, 2027), not waivable for an initial registration, and registration brings the lab under the Quality Management System Regulation (21 CFR 820) and FDA inspection | | $13,785 a year if you register | [21 CFR 807.65 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/807.65); [eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-H/part-807/subpart-B/section-807.65); [FDA K203660 clearance letter](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf20/K203660.pdf) |

**The FDA question you must settle before scaling implant milling.** The 807.65(i) exemption is written for labs that provide a service with previously manufactured devices (a crown milled from a cleared zirconia disc to a dentist's prescription fits that reading), but milling the implant-connecting geometry is where it gets specific. FDA has treated custom abutments milled from titanium pre-milled blanks as requiring a 510(k)-cleared system, with the blank manufacturer designating "validated milling centers" that follow its cleared parameters; a lab milling abutment interfaces from generic discs on its own parameters is manufacturing an uncleared device. Bear Milling's $59 custom abutment and any premill work falls in that category. For TRI, the matrix crown is screw-retained onto TRI's cleared matrix abutment, which is why TRI runs a certification program at all; what you need in writing from TRI is whether certified labs mill under TRI's clearance and on TRI-validated parameters (the "matrix milling strategy support" form you have pending is the right channel). Until that answer is in hand, sell the TRI matrix crown as a TRI-certified workflow, sell implant crowns on stock or OEM abutments and Ti-bases from cleared manufacturers, and do not market "custom abutments milled in house." Separately from the clearance question, TRI's condition that certified labs hold their own FDA establishment registration ([FDA FY2027 fee notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/30/2026-15335/medical-device-user-fee-rates-for-fiscal-year-2027); [FDA MDUFA fee page](https://www.fda.gov/industry/fda-user-fee-programs/medical-device-user-fee-amendments-mdufa-fees)) changes the lab's regulatory posture: a registered contract manufacturer is expected to operate a quality management system under 21 CFR 820 as amended effective February 2, 2026, and can be inspected. That is a deliberate business decision, not a by-product of a certification form, and it is why the plan gates the fee on TRI's written answers. This is labeled as my reading of FDA practice, not legal advice; a one-hour consult with a regulatory attorney or the NADL is worth more than any amount of desk research here.

### B.7 Dallas-Fort Worth and Miami markets

*Scope:* Dallas-Fort Worth (TX) and Miami / South Florida as candidate remote-served markets for Integra Dental Design (Bellevue, WA): dentist and lab supply, full-arch/DSO demand structure, state lab regulation, tax and entity costs, labor, real estate, flights and parcel logistics; conclusion on obtainable accounts in 12 months

**Agent summary.** Bottom line: neither Dallas-Fort Worth nor South Florida gives a Bellevue lab a structural edge over local labs or national mail-order labs. Of the two, DFW is the less-bad option, and only as a lab-to-lab milling and equipment-dealer play; South Florida has no structural case and should be deprioritized behind Southern California, where Integra already has accounts and family presence. FACTS. (1) Regulation is asymmetric. Texas Occupations Code 266.151 requires a registration certificate for any person who "provide[s] or offer[s] to provide dental laboratory services," and 266.153 covers "each dental laboratory doing business in this state"; nothing in ch. 266, 22 TAC ch. 116, or TSBDE's "Do I Need a Dental Laboratory Registration" chart exempts an out-of-state lab, and 266.102(b)(5)(D)-(E) gives the Board rulemaking power over prescriptions referred to out-of-state labs. Registration is $125 initial / $136 annual (TSBDE 2022 application form; current schedule page would not render) but requires a Certified Dental Technician "employed by and working on the premises" at least 30 hours/week (22 TAC 116.5) and a jurisprudence assessment. The brief does not say whether Integra employs a CDT; if it does not, Texas is a compliance problem before it is a sales problem. Florida is the opposite: Fla. Stat. 466.032(5)(d)2 states an out-of-state lab serving a Florida dentist "is not required to register"; voluntary registration costs $200 (Board page) with 18 CE hours biennially. Both states exempt dental prostheses from sales tax (Tex. Tax Code 151.313(a)(5) "dental, or prosthetic device"; Fla. Stat. 212.08(2)(a) "dentures... prosthetic and orthopedic appliances," Rule 12A-1.021). Foreign-LLC registration is $750 in Texas (BOC 4.152(3) via 4.154) plus an annual franchise report (no tax below the $2.65M 2026-27 threshold) and $125 plus $138.75/yr in Florida. (2) Lab supply is the decisive asymmetry. Census CBP 2023 (via two secondary tabulations) shows Florida with 424 dental-lab establishments, second in the nation, versus Texas 277 and Washington 175. BLS OEWS May 2023 shows the Miami metro with 800 dental lab technicians at a location quotient of 1.31 versus DFW 670 at 0.75 (Texas statewide LQ 0.57, Florida 1.12). South Florida is over-supplied with lab capacity relative to the U.S.; DFW is under-supplied and already imports lab work.
