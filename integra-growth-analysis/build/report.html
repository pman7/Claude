1. The direct recommendation

Grow in place. Spend the next 6 to 12 months turning Integra into the implant and full-arch lab of record for restorative practices within about 25 minutes of 35 148th Ave SE, with a trial case as the only offer. Finish the TRI matrix certification training, which is free, but do not pay the $13,785 FDA establishment registration TRI requires of certified labs until TRI answers four questions in writing (Section 12.2). Do not sign a lease anywhere. Do not open an office in Orange County or Maui. Keep the ARUM dealership alive as an opportunistic, reply-within-24-hours business, not as the plan.

Why this and not geographic expansion:

  1. The constraint is not capacity or geography. It is founder attention, cash and delivery reliability. Your own August 2026 reconstruction shows 519 billed units in twelve months, roughly a day and a half of milling per month, two clients at 91 percent of lab revenue, cash that touched $4.73 on March 31 and $2,421 on August 27, and 6 of 13 active cases late or past their ship date. Every expansion model that adds a lease, a hire or a travel schedule makes all three constraints worse before it makes anything better.
  2. No lease-based option survives the cash test. At $5,000, $15,000 or $30,000 of expansion cash, every office, satellite, showroom or production option in the model is infeasible on either cash-to-open or worst-case drawdown, and all of them are negative in the base case after owner time (Section 8, Section 11). The only options that fit the envelopes are the ones that use the mills, people and relationships you already pay for.
  3. The Puget Sound market you are standing in is the richest one on the list for your specific capabilities. King County had 112 licensed dentists per 100,000 residents in 2023, the highest ratio in Washington by a wide margin (every other region is under 70; UW Center for Health Workforce Studies, state license data), a dense Eastside implant and All-on-X practice base, Seattle Study Club's headquarters in Kirkland, a TRI user and referral source already on your books (Smiles By Design), and a landlord who is also your biggest client and a UW faculty member. You have not yet worked this market with the discipline the Eight Dentists plan laid out in August.
  4. Every distant market has a structural problem you cannot fix with an office. South Orange County sits in the most lab-dense county in the most lab-dense state in the country (California has 734 dental lab establishments, Census CBP 2023), with Glidewell headquartered in Irvine and TRI USA in Santa Ana. Maui has about 116 licensed dentists, no standalone commercial lab that desk research could find, but 25-plus labs one inter-island flight away in Honolulu and mainland labs already shipping in on a 2-day commitment. Anchorage is served overnight from Seattle and has the highest dentist density in the US, but it also has at least five local labs, two of them digital. Dallas and Miami have no structural reason to prefer a Bellevue lab at all.
  5. The two highest-value things you can sell are already in the building: a full-arch zirconia protocol with a documented slow-cooldown fix, and an implant crown package at $269 to $299 against Glidewell's $454 to $486 custom-abutment crown. Neither has been sold systematically. The All-on-X lines on your fee schedule have never been billed.

The strongest argument against this recommendation. The lab's unit economics are small. At $133 average per unit and about $11,700 a month collected, even a well-executed local push gets the lab to perhaps $17,000 to $22,000 a month in twelve months, which covers the business and starts to pay you, but does not change your life. Equipment does: one ARUM sale carries roughly $17,000 of gross margin, and your dealer rights have no territory restriction. If you believe you can close three to five machines in the next year, the equipment business is worth more than every lab option combined, and "grow in place" looks like a decision to stay small. The honest counter is that seven months of dealership produced zero closes and two stalled quotes, the Q4 plan's own base case was three to five machines with maximal effort, and equipment sales consume the same founder hours that the lab needs. So the recommendation is not "abandon equipment." It is: stop letting equipment prospecting set your calendar, answer inbound equipment heat the same day, close the two quotes already open, and let the lab fund the business while machines remain upside.

A second real objection: "grow in place" has been the plan since August and the pipeline shows a Kirkland list, a Bellevue list, one trial account (Dr. Joe Kim) and one new remote account (Harmony Dental, Eugene). If it has not worked in two months, why would it work now? Because it has not actually been run. The Eight Dentists plan called for eleven calls in one morning, sample arches milled that week and four drop-offs a day. The Maui trip, the Buffalo trip, the Q4 equipment plan and the TRI onboarding happened instead. Section 14 turns the plan into a 90-day schedule with weekly numbers and gates so that you can tell by week 6 whether it is working.

Three compliance facts surfaced by this research that change the plan more than any market does. (1) TRI requires certified manufacturing centers to hold their own FDA establishment registration as a contract manufacturer (your own Notion task, October 7, 2026); the FY2027 annual fee is $13,785 and the initial registration cannot be waived (FDA FY2027 fee notice). That turns the TRI option from a $2,400 wedge into a $19,000 first-year commitment and moves it from second to ninth in the ranking; it is still worth doing once TRI's referral volume is known, and not before. (2) Washington requires every dental laboratory to register with the Department of Health and, for registrations issued after January 31, 2025, to employ a Certified Dental Technician working at least 30 hours a week or operate under a licensed dentist's supervision (RCW 70.352.050). You are registered (DLAB.FS.70096007, effective September 8, 2025); your CDT, Young Kim, is scheduled for 20 hours a week. Either the registration rests on dentist supervision, which should be documented, or Young's hours need to reach 30, which also happens to be the design capacity the growth plan needs. (3) Oregon now requires any lab preparing work from an Oregon dentist's work order to hold a Health Licensing Office authorization ($250 application plus $500 authorization, renewing at $750 a year, with the same 30-hour CDT rule and material and point-of-origin disclosures); enforcement discretion ends October 31, 2026 (Oregon HLO permanent rule HLO 4-2026, HLO program page). Harmony Dental in Eugene is an Oregon account.

3. How the options were evaluated

Fourteen geography / service / customer combinations were modeled the same way:

  • Operating models compared separately for each geography where they apply: remote service from Bellevue; periodic sales visits and partnerships; a client consultation / pickup / case-coordination office; a staffed finishing and QC satellite; a production lab; an equipment demonstration and training location. A mailing address or coworking desk is not treated as a production lab anywhere in this document, and the marketing implications of each footprint are noted.
  • Costs are itemized low / base / high in model/inputs.json: rent, NNN or operating pass-throughs, deposits (shown separately and included in cash required), build-out, utilities, internet, insurance, permits, entity registration and franchise taxes, staffing with payroll load, owner time at an explicit opportunity cost of $40 / $60 / $100 an hour, travel (flights, cars, lodging, meals), shipping both ways by lane, remakes, payment fees and marketing. Bellevue's existing overhead is excluded to avoid double counting. Equipment purchase and financing are shown as alternatives, not added together, and no financing approval is assumed.
  • Demand is modeled as accounts ramping linearly to a 12-month count, times units per account per month by product, times contribution per unit on that market's shipping lane. Formulas are in Section 8.
  • Ranking weights year-1 profit after owner time (25), cash to open plus 90 days (20), month-12 cash flow per owner hour (15), exit exposure (10), capability fit (15), evidence quality (10) and speed to first revenue (5). Three alternative weightings test sensitivity (Section 11).

Taxes and entity rules that an office triggers. California treats dental laboratories as retailers: "Tax applies to their entire charges for such products regardless of whether a separate charge or billing is made for materials and manufacturing services" (CDTFA Regulation 1506(e), Business Taxes Law Guide revision 2025, fact). A physical office creates nexus, so every California sale would carry Orange County's 7.75 percent sales tax. California labs already charge it, so dentists will accept it on the invoice, but it is a compliance layer (seller's permit, quarterly returns) you do not have today. "Every LLC that is doing business or organized in California must pay an annual tax of $800"; the first-year exemption applied only to tax years 2021 through 2023 (FTB, updated Mar 5, 2026, fact). Registering Nova Dental LLC as a foreign LLC (Form LLC-5) is $70 plus a $20 Statement of Information. Without an office, a Washington lab shipping into California stays below the $500,000 economic-nexus threshold and owes nothing in California. One nuance from CDTFA Regulation 1591: crowns, bridges, dentures and inlays are explicitly taxable, but dental implant systems including abutments sold to a dentist for treatment are exempt medicines, so a separately stated abutment line would be exempt (Reg. 1591). California has no dental-lab registration statute; labs operate under the Dental Practice Act exemption in Business and Professions Code 1626(e) with a dentist's signed written authorization (agent finding, medium confidence).

6. Why a customer would switch to you, and what stops them

Dentist-facing lab demand. Dentists switch labs on fit and remake pain, turnaround misses, communication, and a specific clinical problem solved; price alone moves the bottom of the market (see the switching evidence the agents compiled in Appendix B when it lands; the pattern is consistent across ADA, LMT and Dentaltown sources). Your levers, in order of credibility: (1) a sample arch and a documented protocol in the doctor's hand; (2) an implant package 30 to 40 percent under Glidewell with a fit guarantee; (3) a 10-minute drive and a technician who will come to the chair; (4) TRI certification for the TRI users. The barriers: a bad first case (your late-ship rate), the office manager who owns the lab relationship, existing lab programs bundled with implant companies, and the fact that most practices use two labs and will give you the overflow first. Expect a 15 to 25 percent trial-to-recurring conversion on warm introductions and well under 10 percent on cold outreach (estimate; your own Dr. Joe Kim and Harmony Dental experience is the local evidence).

Lab-to-lab milling demand. Labs switch milling partners on price, cutoff time and whether the milling center competes for their doctors. Your levers: a Pacific cutoff, "we never contact your doctors," no design or intake fee, and a sintering protocol for arches. Barriers: Haus and Alien are cheaper on crowns, every lab already has a milling partner, and your own ops record (late cases) is the opposite of what a milling customer buys. Conversion from a first case is high (labs are rational buyers) if the first case ships on time.

Equipment and software demand. Labs and practices buy mills on trust in the installer, financing, and a deadline. Your levers: operator credibility, dealer rights across brands (ARUM, MillBox, hyperDENT), and the Section 179 calendar (2026 limit $2,560,000 with a $4,090,000 phase-out and permanent 100 percent bonus depreciation for property acquired after January 19, 2025; the asset must be placed in service by December 31 for a 2026 deduction, Block Advisors summary of Rev. Proc. 2025-32 and the 2025 Act, confirm with the buyer's CPA). Barriers: Axsys's service depth, ARUM USA selling in its own backyard, your lack of a stocked spare-parts kit and a second installer, and the cash float on financed deals (Ascentium and Geneva fund at acceptance, after installation).

8.1 Formulas (implemented in model/model.py)

  • Accounts in month t = A12 × min(1, t / ramp months), where A12 is the 12-month account count (low / base / high) and ramp is 6 to 12 months.
  • Units in month t by product = accounts(t) × units per account per month for that product (the "case mix" in inputs.json; for option A the base mix per account is 6 zirconia crowns, 1.5 implant crowns, 0.15 full arches and 0.8 night guards a month).
  • Revenue(t) = Σ units × price.
  • Contribution per unit = price − materials − labor hours × loaded technician rate ($32 / $40 / $48) − round-trip shipping on that market's lane ÷ units per shipment − payment fee (0 / 3 / 3.5 percent) − remake allowance (3 / 5 / 8 percent of price). Pessimistic contribution uses the low price with high costs; optimistic the reverse.
  • Option cash flow(t) = Σ units × contribution − monthly option cash cost. Fully loaded cash flow subtracts owner hours × $40 / $60 / $100.
  • Break-even accounts = monthly option cash cost ÷ contribution per account (cash basis), or (monthly cash + owner time) ÷ contribution per account (fully loaded).
  • Payback month = first month in which cumulative option cash flow covers cash to open. Maximum drawdown = the most negative cumulative cash position in the twelve months. "First positive month" is the first month the fully loaded monthly figure is positive.
  • Capacity check: Σ units × labor hours ≤ 220 spare production hours a month (Young about 80 design hours, Edward about 160 CAM and mill hours, part-time finishing about 120 hours, less about 70 hours used by current work; estimate). Spindle time is not binding at roughly 90 percent idle.

Direct labor is charged per unit at the loaded technician rate; your own hours are charged separately at the opportunity-cost rate and never double counted with labor. Bellevue overhead is not allocated.
